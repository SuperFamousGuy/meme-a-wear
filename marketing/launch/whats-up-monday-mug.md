## Audience

The mug is for the older end of the "What's Up Brah?" segment in
`marketing/target-market.md`: ages 13 to 35, South Park fans and gamers
(confidence `low`, one brief). The cross-trend claims that matter here:

- Mug buying is `contested`. Mugs look stronger for older buyers (mid-20s to
  mid-30s) and as gifts. Aim posts at working adults and office gift buyers,
  not teens.
- The humor is ironic and absurd (`high`). Loud, goofy bravado lands
  (`medium`).

What makes them share: Monday is a shared enemy. A Monday-morning face-off
gets tagged to a coworker or friend ("this is you"). The caption swaps "brah"
for "Monday", so fans connect it to the meme through the stance. Non-fans
still get a Monday joke, which helps the mug outlast the meme.

## Posts

- Never use "Randy", "Marsh", "South Park", "Sharon", "Broadway Bro Down",
  "Comedy Central", or "bro down" in captions, hashtags, on-screen text, or
  alt text. Never name or tag the meme's creator or uploaders.

  of the Mondays".

**Success:** 1 or more orders by 2026-11-04, and favorites at 5% or more.
Favorites matter more for a mug, because gift buyers save items before the
holidays.

**Pull the product** when any of these happens:

- Etsy or a rights holder sends an IP complaint. Deactivate at once.
- The brief turns `dead` or `do-not-use`.
- By 2026-11-30, the listing has no orders and fewer than 75 views. The
  later date gives the Monday and gift angles time to work after the meme
  fades.

**First small ad test signal:** 2 or more organic orders, or 10 or more
favorites, by 2026-11-16, while holiday gift search runs. The ad budget is $0,
so this is only a signal. The user decides whether to fund a test.
