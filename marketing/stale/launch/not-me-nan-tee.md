## Audience

The buyers are the Nan segment in `marketing/target-market.md`: UK-leaning,
ages 16 to 40, on TikTok, plus dog owners (confidence: low, one brief). They
sit in the older half of the contested age range. That makes them more
likely than the Losercore segment to buy for themselves and to buy gifts.
The tone is deadpan British understatement.

Owners share clips of their own dog dressed as Nan (low). The tee rides on
that habit. The best post puts the tee next to a real dog in a headscarf.
The joke is the impostor, so posts state the obvious with a straight face.

## Channels

1. **TikTok.** Create an account. The brief lists TikTok first, and dog-granny
   videos carried the spread. The meme is audio-led, and a business account
   likely can't use the skit's sound (Fox Music 2026 guide, checked
   2026-10-18). On-screen text must carry the line.

   Reels travel well there. Reuse the TikTok clips.
3. **Pinterest.** Optional, last. Neither the brief nor the profile shows

## Posts

Use the brand voice with British spelling: deadpan, short, and in on the
joke. Always use the full line. Never shorten or Americanize it. Never name
or tag the skit's creator, the Inbetweeners account, or anyone who posted the
original clips. Don't use the skit's audio.

### TikTok

No clip exists yet. The user films or approves each clip. You can score a
clip with `virality_predictor`. The score is an estimate. Connect the
account and run `tiktok_music_trending` (genre `ALL`, country `GB`) to list
cleared Commercial Music Library sounds. A quiet, unbothered track suits the
deadpan tone.

- **Hook A, the impostor.** Shot: a dog in a headscarf under a duvet, then
  cut to the folded tee. This needs the user's own dog, or a dog whose owner
  agrees. Text on screen: "she said she was nan". Then: "she is
  blatantly just not me nan".
